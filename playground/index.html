Playground

Three small experiments you can poke. Everything runs in your browser on made-up numbers: nothing is sent anywhere.

1. What does a correlation look like?

Each dot is one measurement of two things. The number r says how closely they move together: +1 in lockstep, 0 unrelated, -1 opposite. R² is r squared: the share of the wiggle one explains about the other.

Try 15 dots with a true link of 0: you will often see a "pattern" that is only luck. That is why the dashboard reports an interval, not just r.

2. Two kinds of bands around a trend line

The straight line is the best fit through the dots. The narrow band says where the true line probably is. The wide band says where new dots should land. More dots shrink the narrow band; they do nothing to the wide one, because noise is noise.

The true slope is 0.5. Watch whether the interval for the slope contains it, and how its width falls as you add dots.

3. Choosing where to aim when testing costs time

Each of the 781 aims has a hidden damage score. You may test only a few. The bars are the true scores; the coloured ticks show which aims each strategy tested. Regret = best possible minus best found (lower is better). The "hunch" strategy uses a quick, imperfect guess to decide what to test, like the learned scorer in the real project.

What to look for: random tests are the worst. "Zoom in" strategies need enough tests for the first pass to land near a peak, so with very few tests plain even spacing can beat them, and with plenty they win easily. A decent hunch wins at every budget, but a useless one (quality 0) is worse than random: it spends its zoom-in budget around an arbitrary spot. This is a toy: the real board also has 781 aims, but the real scores are not smooth bumps.

Lend your processor

Run a small, synthetic experiment in your browser, and, only if you choose, share the answer. Your device does the arithmetic; nothing leaves it unless you tap Share.

Is it safe? What exactly happens?

  • It only runs while this page is open. No install, no account, no permissions, no camera, microphone, location or files. It stops if you leave, lock the screen or press Stop.
  • The page cannot send data anywhere. Its security policy blocks every connection except to this site itself. The only way anything leaves your device is you tapping Share, which opens a public GitHub page that you then submit (or you copy the text yourself).
  • What a result contains: a work-unit number, four whole numbers (the experiment's totals) and a checksum. No device details, no name, no location.
  • Nobody has to trust you or me. Every unit is deterministic: anyone can recompute it and check it. Wrong or made-up results are rejected automatically.
  • The cost is battery and warmth. The default is Gentle (about a quarter of one core), the session stops after 5 minutes unless you choose otherwise, and it pauses when this tab is hidden or the battery is low (where your browser reports it). If your phone feels warm, press Pause.
  • What it is for: a made-up version of the aim-search game: how much does a decent hunch help, depending on how many tests you can afford and how good the hunch is? It does not run the real game and is not a serious research workload. Read the honest details.

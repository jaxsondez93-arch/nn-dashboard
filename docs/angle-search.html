Does splitting the angle in half find the best shot?

A reader proposed a faster way to aim than brute force: shoot the two ends of the valid range, keep whichever did better, then try two points straddling it, narrowing each round. Tested here directly against the day's puzzle board, with the same real physics engine the project uses everywhere, against a fine 341-point sweep of the same board (the closest thing to "every angle" that's practical to compute).

True best (fine sweep)27° → 8 of 15
Bisection search landed on50.8° → 6 of 15
Rounds / shots used7 rounds, 14 shots
Full sweep — damage at every 0.5°
Bisection search — each shot, in order
Why it missed the peak: the real damage curve isn't a smooth hill with one summit — it's mostly flat at zero, with a few narrow, spiky pockets where a shot actually threads the blocks. The true best (27°) sits inside the first pocket, right next to where the search started. But round 1 compared 16° (0 damage) against 55.75° (4 damage) and moved toward 55.75° — stepping over the 27° pocket without ever landing in it, and the method has no way to look back once it's committed to a side. Narrowing in on a winner works well for a single smooth peak; this board's landscape has several separate ones, and a few extra sample points chosen by halving isn't enough to tell which of the flat-zero stretches is hiding one.
